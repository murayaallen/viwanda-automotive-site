Viwanda is Swahili for industries. The company is literally named Industry Automotive, and the name describes the job: the same skilled hands this country has always had, now working to a documented standard.

Most Kenyans learned to drive on a car kept alive by a fundi who knew it by ear. That skill is real and this workshop does not sneer at it. What it adds is the part jua kali was never set up to give you: a written quote, a photograph of the fault before anyone touches it, and a finish that stands up next to a showroom car.

We work mostly in paint. Complete resprays, two-tone and pearl finishes, glass coating, accident and panel repair, and full restorations where the shell is worth saving. We import parts for any model, and we detail — buffing, valeting and tint.

The workshop is in Garden Estate, off Thika Road. Insurers, fleets and corporates send vehicles here, and so do people who simply want their own car back the way it looked.

Ahadi What we hold to

Four things, every job.

  1. 01

    A written figure first.

    Bei kwanza

    Nothing is touched until you have agreed a price in writing. A figure given before anyone has looked at the car is a guess.

  2. 02

    Photographed at every stage.

    Picha kila hatua

    Strip, panel, fill, mask, prime, colour, coat. You see the work rather than take it on trust, and an assessor sees the same pictures you do.

  3. 03

    Bad news early.

    Ukweli mapema

    If stripping a panel shows something worse underneath, you hear about it before we carry on — not on the invoice.

  4. 04

    Built for this road.

    Barabara yetu

    Intervals set by matuta, dust and Thika Road traffic, and paint that has to survive Nairobi sun — not a manual written for a motorway.
